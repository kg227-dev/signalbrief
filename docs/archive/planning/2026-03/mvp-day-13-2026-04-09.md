**Day 13 is operationally healthy and closer to the exit bar, but still not exit-green.** The run delivered 35/35 items, 7/7 topics full, 7/7 topics above depth-15, 610 total candidates after story-relationship filtering, and 698/698 broker fetch items passed across 45 domains. There was no discovery contribution in this run; the entire pool came from the broker path.

**Trusted share improved, but not enough.** The final selected set rose to 25/35 trusted T1/T2 items (71.4%), up from Day 12's 22/35 (62.9%), but still below the 80% MVP target. The selected mix was 25 trusted items and 10 weaker items, split across 20 strong, 5 premium, 7 standard, and 3 unknown-tier selections.

**The writeup gate is now the primary blocker.** The run attempted 178 writeups and dropped 143 of them, all classified under `validator_mismatch`. First-pass success was only 15/178 (8.4%), repairs succeeded 19/112 times (17.0%), and yet the run still filled all 35 slots because the pools were deep enough to keep backfilling. That means the product is mechanically resilient but still choosing around a broken writeup validator.

   All 698 broker fetch items passed, spanning 45 domains, with no evidence of fetch, scheduler, or freshness-pipeline failure. This is the second straight day where retrieval volume was not the limiting factor.

3. **Trusted share recovered in four topics.**
   Financial Services, Technology, Energy, and Life Sciences all finished 5/5 trusted. Healthcare landed at 4/5 trusted and still produced a coherent operator-facing set.

### Hypothesis: what's driving Day 13 results

1. **Retrieval is no longer the bottleneck.**
   The system had enough depth, full broker pass-through, and no underfills. The remaining gap is downstream of retrieval.

2. **The writeup validator is rejecting too aggressively across good source material.**
   Day 13's `validator_mismatch` wall suggests the contract between generation and validation is misaligned. The evidence points to schema-tightness and phrasing checks blocking otherwise usable summaries rather than a content-supply problem.

3. **Backfill ordering is still too tolerant of weak survivors after writeup loss.**
   When strong items fail writeup, Industrials and Consumer & Retail still fall through to standard or unknown-tier survivors instead of recovering with stronger same-topic reserves.

4. **Technology relevance improved once the stronger candidates survived.**
   That makes Technology less of a ranking crisis than Day 12 and more of a proof that the system can recover when the writeup gate stops deleting good options.

### Fix plan

1. **Fix the generation-validator contract first.**
   Sample Day 13 `validator_mismatch` outputs across Financial Services, Healthcare, Industrials, and Consumer & Retail. Classify whether the failures are caused by sentence-shape rules, missing mechanism requirements, or over-strict concision checks, then relax or rewrite the validator and prompt together.

2. **Add same-topic trusted backfill before weak-tier fallthrough.**
   If a topic still has trusted candidates after a writeup failure, standard and unknown-tier reserves should not win by default. This matters most for Industrials and Consumer & Retail.

3. **Instrument writeup-failure slices by topic and source tier.**
   Day 13 shows that aggregate writeup counts are no longer enough. Emit audit-ready breakdowns so it is obvious when one validator rule is deleting an entire topic's strong pool.

4. **Keep Technology relevance changes in place and re-check tomorrow.**
   Technology moved back toward operator-facing stories. Preserve that path and verify it stays stable on the next weekday run.
