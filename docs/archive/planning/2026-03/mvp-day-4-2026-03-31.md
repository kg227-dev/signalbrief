### Hypothesis: what's driving Day 4 results

1. **Tuesday morning run captures full Monday publishing cycle.** The 48h lookback at Tuesday 07:00 ET includes all Monday content. This explains the 344-candidate pool vs Day 3's 77 — Monday trade publishing is the dominant input, not the Tuesday morning window.
2. **Source cap discipline is the primary trusted-share constraint.** With americanbanker.com capped at 3/3, modernhealthcare.com at 3/3, and freightwaves.com at 3/3, the selection must draw from standard-tier sources. Caps are intentional diversity policy, but trusted share won't reliably reach 80% unless the strong-tier source pool per topic is at least 5–6 deep.
3. **Cross-topic contamination is persistent and scorer-driven.** STAT News, The Register, and arstechnica.com publish across multiple verticals. Their authority scores are high, so off-topic stories from these sources beat weaker on-topic stories. The scorer needs topic-fit weighting to complement source-authority weighting.
4. **Stale-heavy sources (energy_canary, industrials_area_development) contribute noise but not signal.** 97–96% stale rates suggest these sources are primarily archival/low-publication-frequency. They pad the fetch count but rarely produce fresh candidates. Consider increasing the freshness filter or deprioritizing these sources.
